Proof overview
==============

Let :math:`T` be a nondegenerate spherical tetrahedron and :math:`R(T)` an
elementary Regge mate.  The proof compares not only the volumes or Dehn
invariants of the two tetrahedra, but their universal framed quadric classes.

The argument has four transitions.

Angle geometry to algebraic parameters
--------------------------------------

Exponentiating the six dihedral angles turns the additive Regge formulas into
a monomial involution.  The determinant of the angle Gram matrix is invariant,
and its square root gives an orientation cover.  Two explicit rational charts
cover every physical tetrahedron/Regge pair.

Parameters to a three-weight object
-----------------------------------

Over a suitable Regge-stable open set :math:`U`, the Gram quadric and its four
coordinate facets define one alternating relative motive.  Splitting its
quadric strata gives exactly three weight levels:

.. math::

   \mathbb Q(0),\qquad
   \mathbb Q(1)^{\oplus 5}\oplus\chi(1),\qquad
   \mathbb Q(2).

The Artin character :math:`\chi` records the half-phase that cannot be chosen
rationally on :math:`U`.

Coproduct equality to class equality
------------------------------------

The reduced coproduct records six length/angle channels.  Five are ordinary
Tate channels, and one is the :math:`\chi`-channel.  The Regge involution
preserves their sum.  A three-level lemma therefore places the difference of
the two framed classes in :math:`H^1(U,\mathbb Q(2))`.  The stated
Garkusha--Borel input makes that group zero.

Universal equality to finite equidecomposition
----------------------------------------------

Specialization gives equality for the chosen tetrahedron.  Goncharov's
weight-two comparison and spherical map send this equality to the spherical
scissors group.  Standard rational-vector and suspension results then remove
the two intermediate weakenings and yield finite scissors congruence.
