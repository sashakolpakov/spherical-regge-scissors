\section{The theorem and the logical route}
\label{cp:sec-statement}

\subsection{Spherical scissors congruence}

Let \(S^3\subset\R^4\) be the unit sphere.  A spherical polytope is a
finite union of geodesic simplices contained in open hemispheres.  The
spherical scissors group \(\Pfull(S^3)\) is generated by symbols \([P]\),
one for each such polytope, subject to
\[
 [P]=[P_1]+\cdots+[P_m]
\]
when the \(P_i\) have disjoint interiors and union \(P\), and
\([P]=[gP]\) for every spherical isometry \(g\).  Thus an equality in
\(\Pfull(S^3)\) is the stable algebraic form of equidecomposability.  A
cancellation theorem recalled in Section~\ref{cp:sec-specialization} turns
the equality proved below into an actual finite equidecomposition.

Label the vertices of a nondegenerate spherical tetrahedron by
\(0,1,2,3\), and select the opposite edge pair \(01,23\).  The elementary
Regge operation fixes the two corresponding edge lengths and replaces the
remaining four lengths
\(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_4\) by
\begin{equation}
 \ell_i^R=\sigma_\ell-\ell_i,
 \qquad
 \sigma_\ell=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^4\ell_j.
 \label{cp:eq-regge-lengths}
\end{equation}
For a genuine mate, the same formula holds for the corresponding four
dihedral angles, with their half-sum in place of \(\sigma_\ell\).  This is
the spherical Regge theorem of Akopyan--Izmestiev
\cite{AkopyanIzmestiev2019}; we state the exact input in
Theorem~\ref{cp:thm-regge-input}.

\begin{theorem}[Spherical Regge scissors congruence]
\label{cp:thm-main}
Let \(T\subset S^3\) be a nondegenerate spherical tetrahedron and let
\(T^R\) be any nondegenerate elementary Regge mate.  Then
\[
 [T]=[T^R]\qquad\text{in }\Pfull(S^3).
\]
Consequently \(T\) and \(T^R\) admit a finite spherical
equidecomposition.
\end{theorem}

No algebraicity assumption is made: the angles and vertices may be
transcendental.

\subsection{What is proved and what is imported}

The proof uses four established inputs.  They are quoted in the precise
forms needed later.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(I\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.7em]
\item \textbf{Regge geometry.}  The same orthogonal linear involution acts
on the four moving edge lengths and on the four moving dihedral angles, and
Regge mates have the same spherical volume
\cite{AkopyanIzmestiev2019}.

\item \textbf{Motivic functoriality and localization.}  Rational
constructible motives over a smooth \(\Q\)-scheme have pullback, tensor
products, internal duals, Tate twists, localization triangles, and the
projective-bundle formula.  We use these operations only for complements
of quadrics and their coordinate sections.  Section
\ref{cp:sec-motivic-dictionary} defines the resulting object explicitly
and lists every formal property used.

\item \textbf{Weight-two rigidity.}  Motivic cohomology in weight two is
birational in degrees at most one, and the corresponding field groups are
unchanged by a purely transcendental extension
\cite{Garkusha2023}.  Borel's theorem gives
\(\gr_2^\gamma K_3(\Q)\otimes\Q=0\) \cite{Borel1977}.

\item \textbf{Goncharov's comparison.}  An oriented quadric with four
ordered hyperplane faces defines a weight-two quadric-scissors class and a
framed relative motive.  Goncharov's canonical map from primitive
weight-two quadric-scissors classes to
\(\gr_2^\gamma K_3(\C)\otimes\Q\) is injective, and his spherical map to
the quadric complex is injective in degree one
\cite[Theorems~4.13 and~4.14]{Goncharov1999}.
\end{enumerate}

Everything else---the parameter space, its rationality, the relative
quadric calculation, the Regge coproduct identity, descent from the one
nonsplit face, specialization, and the final scissors argument---is proved
here.

\subsection{A map of the argument}

The proof has the following seven steps.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.1em]
\item Replace each dihedral angle \(\theta\) by its phase
\(u=e^{\mathrm i\theta}\).  The additive Regge formula then becomes the
monomial substitution \(u_i^R=\tau/u_i\).

\item Adjoin the orientation root \(w=\sqrt{\det G}\) of the angle Gram
matrix.  Two elementary substitutions, \(r=w-sx\) and \(r_+=w+sx\),
rationalize this double cover.  Every physical Regge pair is a complex
point of one of the resulting rational opens \(U\).

\item Associate to the universal Gram matrix and its mate two oriented
quadric simplices over \(U\).  Their normalized relative motives have only
Tate degrees \(0,1,2\).  Five binary face sections split; the sixth is the
common quadratic cover determined by the fixed phase \(p\), whose sign
summand is an Artin motive \(\chi\).

\item The middle coproduct of a dual quadric simplex is
\(\sum_e L_e\otimes A_e\), where \(L_e\) and \(A_e\) are its oriented
length and angle cross-ratios.  The Regge matrix is orthogonal, so the
coproducts of the two mates agree.  Trace from the fixed quadratic cover
shows that this equality already holds over \(U\).

\item A framed three-weight object with zero middle coproduct can differ
from zero only by a direct endpoint extension.  Here that obstruction is
\[
 \Ext^1\bigl(\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2)\bigr)=H^1(U,\Q(2)).
\]
Weight-two birational invariance reduces this group to
\(\gr_2^\gamma K_3(\Q)\otimes\Q=0\).  Hence the universal framed defect
vanishes.

\item Pull the identity back to the complex point representing the given
tetrahedron.  Goncharov's weight-two injectivity turns the motivic identity
into equality of the associated primitive quadric-scissors classes, and
then into equality in the reduced spherical scissors group.

\item The unreduced difference is a suspension of a spherical polygon.
Regge volume equality makes that polygon have area zero; area classifies
spherical planar scissors classes.  Therefore the suspension is zero.
\end{enumerate}

``monomial'' is literal rather than metaphorical.
