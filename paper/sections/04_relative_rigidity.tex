\section{The universal Regge defect and weight-two rigidity}
\label{cp:sec-relative-rigidity}

We now apply the language of Section~\ref{cp:sec-motivic-dictionary} to the
universal pair over the rational open \(U\) constructed in
Section~\ref{cp:sec-phase-charts}.  The argument has two independent parts.
First we calculate the weight pieces of the two relative quadric motives.
Then we calculate their middle coproduct and use a vanishing theorem to show
that no endpoint extension remains.

Fix the physical point chosen in
Proposition~\ref{cp:prop-physical-point-on-chart}.  From now on we replace
\(U\), if necessary, by a smaller Regge-stable principal open which still
contains that point.  We require on this open that every cross-ratio used
below is a regular unit and that the finitely many root covers used to
choose isotropic directions and half-phases are finite \'etale.  At the
physical point the relevant phases are nonzero and the face sections are
smooth, so none of these additional conditions removes the point.  This
shrinking changes neither the function field in
\eqref{cp:eq-universal-open-function-field} nor the birational motivic
cohomology calculation.

\subsection{Why the dual quadrics and all their faces split}

Let \(G\) and \(G^R\) be the two universal angle Gram matrices over \(U\),
with their common determinant root \(w\).  The quadric simplex naturally
attached to \(G\) uses the quadric \(Q_{G^{-1}}\).  Goncharov's projective
duality, which is the antipode in the quadric-scissors Hopf algebra, replaces
it by the coordinate simplex
\begin{equation}
 Q_G:\quad z^tGz=0\subset\mathbf P^3_U
 \label{cp:eq-dual-quadric}
\end{equation}
with the same four ordered coordinate hyperplanes.  It is enough to kill
the dual defect: the antipode is an involution.  We use the dual because its
coordinate sections can be read directly from the entries of \(G\).

A quadratic form over a scheme is called \emph{split} if it is a direct sum
of hyperbolic planes, locally represented by matrices
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).  A split
quadric surface is fibrewise \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\), and its two
projections are its two rulings by lines.

\begin{lemma}[Splitting of the universal coordinate strata]
\label{cp:lem-split-strata}
The quadrics \(Q_G\) and \(Q_{G^R}\) are split over \(U\).  Every coordinate
plane section is a split conic.  Of the six coordinate line sections, five
are split degree-two schemes; the remaining section is the common finite
\'{e}tale cover \(U_p\to U\) from \eqref{cp:eq-p-cover}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a unit \(u\), put \(g=-(u+u^{-1})/2\).  The vector \((1,u)\) is
isotropic for the binary form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&g\\g&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
because \(1+2gu+u^2=0\).  In particular, the binary block of \(G\)
controlled by the phase \(q\) is a hyperbolic plane.

Orthogonally eliminate this nondegenerate binary block in the rank-four
quadratic bundle.  On the remaining two coordinate directions the
restricted form is its binary Schur complement \(C\).  This complement is
oriented by the ordered trivial bundle and the ordered isotropic basis of
the \(q\)-plane.  In the notation of
Section~\ref{cp:sec-phase-charts},
\[
 \det G=w^2,
 \qquad
 \det\begin{pmatrix}1&y\\y&1\end{pmatrix}=1-y^2=-s^2.
\]
The determinant formula for a Schur complement therefore gives
\[
 \det C=-\left(\frac ws\right)^2.
\]
Write \(t=w/s\), a unit on \(U\).  In an oriented local basis of the
complement put
\[
 J_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad J=t^{-1}J_0C.
\]
For every symmetric binary matrix \(C\),
\((J_0C)^2=-\det(C)I\).  Hence \(J^2=I\), and a direct multiplication gives
\(J^tC+CJ=0\).  Also \(\operatorname{tr}J=0\), so the two idempotents
\((1\pm J)/2\) have rank one and their images are line bundles.  If
\(Jv=\pm v\), the skew-adjoint identity gives
\(C(v,v)=-C(Jv,Jv)=-C(v,v)\), so each eigenline is isotropic.
Nondegeneracy of \(C\) then pairs the two eigenlines perfectly.  Thus the
complement, and
hence the rank-four form, is hyperbolic.  This proves that \(Q_G\) is split.
The mate has the same \(q,s,w\), so the identical argument applies to
\(Q_{G^R}\).

Every coordinate rank-three restriction contains a binary block controlled
by at least one of \(a,b,c,d,q\).  It therefore has an isotropic line.  A
smooth conic with a rational point is split, proving the plane-section
claim.  A coordinate rank-two restriction has two isotropic points.  The
phases \(a,b,c,d,q\) display those points for five restrictions.  The last
restriction is defined by \(p+p^{-1}=-2x\), and its two isotropic points are
defined precisely over \(U_p\).  Regge fixes \(p,q\) and replaces the other
four phases by \(\tau/a,\tau/b,\tau/c,\tau/d\), so the same description
holds for the mate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The three weight pieces}

For a coordinate line \(M_I\cong\mathbf P^1_U\), let
\(D_I=Q_G\cap M_I\).  It is a finite \'{e}tale scheme of degree two.  Its
motive has a constant part and a reduced part.  With \(\eta\) denoting the
unit and \(\Tr\) the trace, define the latter as the idempotent image
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde M(D_I)
 =\operatorname{im}\left(1-\frac12\eta\Tr:M(D_I)\longrightarrow M(D_I)
                  \right).
 \label{cp:eq-reduced-artin}
\end{equation}
If \(D_I=U\amalg U\), this is a copy of the constant motive \(\Q_U\),
carrying the difference of the two points.  For the nonsplit \(p\)-section
it is the sign Artin motive \(\chi\) of the double cover \(U_p/U\).

\begin{proposition}[Weight calculation for the quadric face motive]
\label{cp:prop-weight-calculation}
For \(Q=Q_G\) or \(Q_{G^R}\), the normalized relative motive
\(\mathfrak m_U(Q,M)\) of Definition~\ref{cp:def-face-motive} has a
three-step Postnikov tower with pure cones
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \gr_F^0\mathfrak m_U(Q,M)&=\Q_U(0),\\
  \gr_F^1\mathfrak m_U(Q,M)&=
       \bigoplus_{|I|=2}\widetilde M(D_I)(1),\\
  \gr_F^2\mathfrak m_U(Q,M)&=\Q_U(2).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{cp:eq-three-graded-pieces}
\end{equation}
After the gluing in Lemma~\ref{cp:lem-three-weight-rigidity}, the motive lies
in the resulting heart and this tower is its strict three-step filtration.
Here \(F^0\supset F^1\supset F^2\supset F^3=0\) is the decreasing
Tate-degree filtration: \(\Q_U(2)=F^2\) is the bottom subobject and
\(\Q_U(0)=F^0/F^1\) is the top quotient.
Thus the middle piece is a sum of five copies of \(\Q_U(1)\) and one copy
of \(\chi(1)\).  The ruling selected by \(w\) gives the
\(\Q_U(0)\)-frame, and the orientation of the four ordered faces gives the
\(\Q_U(2)\)-frame.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We give the calculation because it is the only place where the geometric
face complex is converted into weights.  Localization and the
projective-bundle formula give
\begin{align}
 M_U(\mathbf P^3_U\setminus Q)
   &\simeq \Q_U\oplus\Q_U(2)[3],
 \label{cp:eq-motive-open-quadric}\\
 M_U(\mathbf P^2_U\setminus C)&\simeq\Q_U
   &&(C\text{ a split smooth conic}),
 \label{cp:eq-motive-open-conic}\\
 M_U(\mathbf P^1_U\setminus D_I)
   &\simeq\Q_U\oplus\widetilde M(D_I)(1)[1],
 \label{cp:eq-motive-punctured-line}\\
 M_U(\mathbf P^0_U)&\simeq\Q_U.
 \label{cp:eq-motive-point}
\end{align}
Here is the relative calculation behind these formulas.  A split quadric
surface has the projective-bundle motive
\[
 M_U(Q)=\Q_U\oplus\Q_U(1)[2]^{\oplus2}\oplus\Q_U(2)[4].
\]
In the Gysin map to \(M_U(\mathbf P^3_U)\), the two middle ruling classes
map by their sum to the unique \(\Q_U(2)[4]\)-class; their difference is
the primitive class.  The bottom and top maps are multiplication by the
nonzero geometric degrees and split over \(\Q\).  The localization
triangle therefore leaves exactly \(\Q_U\) and the primitive
\(\Q_U(2)[3]\).  The same argument for a split conic cancels its two
nonconstant projective-bundle terms.  For
\(D_I\subset\mathbf P^1_U\), the trace summand of \(M_U(D_I)\) maps by
degree \(2\), while the reduced idempotent summand maps to zero; shifting
the latter across the localization triangle gives
\(\widetilde M(D_I)(1)[1]\).  Thus the displayed decompositions are
relative over \(U\), not merely fibrewise identities.

For example, the localization triangle for the two-point divisor
\(D_I\subset\mathbf P^1_U\) splits its motive into the trace part and the
reduced summand \eqref{cp:eq-reduced-artin}.  In the conic and binary
localization triangles, the map on the constant degree-two part is
multiplication by the geometric degree \(2\), and is therefore invertible
over \(\Q\).  The rank-one primitive summand in
\eqref{cp:eq-motive-open-quadric} is the difference of the two ruling
classes; the orientation root \(w\) selects its generator, hence its sign
and frame.

Apply the dual-and-twist operation
\[
 D_2(-)=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(-,\Q_U)(2)
\]
appearing in Definition~\ref{cp:def-face-motive}.  Trace self-duality of a
degree-two \'{e}tale scheme gives
\begin{align*}
 D_2\bigl(M_U(\mathbf P^3_U\setminus Q)\bigr)&\simeq
      \Q_U(2)\oplus\Q_U(0)[-3],\\
 D_2\bigl(M_U(\mathbf P^2_U\setminus C)\bigr)&\simeq\Q_U(2),\\
 D_2\bigl(M_U(\mathbf P^1_U\setminus D_I)\bigr)&\simeq
      \Q_U(2)\oplus\widetilde M(D_I)(1)[-1],\\
 D_2\bigl(M_U(\mathbf P^0_U)\bigr)&\simeq\Q_U(2).
\end{align*}
The cohomological bookkeeping is short.  The exceptional ambient summand
occurs in face-complex degree three, so totalization changes
\(\Q_U(0)[-3]\) to \(\Q_U(0)\).  Each exceptional line summand occurs in
degree one, changing \(\widetilde M(D_I)(1)[-1]\) to
\(\widetilde M(D_I)(1)\).  The remaining constant summands are handled by
their cellular differential, as follows.
The constant \(\Q_U(2)\)-summands form the dual unaugmented cellular
complex of the ordered tetrahedron,
\[
 \Q_U(2)\longrightarrow\Q_U(2)^4\longrightarrow
 \Q_U(2)^6\longrightarrow\Q_U(2)^4.
\]
It has one surviving copy of \(\Q_U(2)\); all other constant terms cancel.
The face degrees cancel the displayed shifts on the exceptional ambient and
line summands.  The resulting Postnikov tower therefore has the three cones
in \eqref{cp:eq-three-graded-pieces}.  Lemma
\ref{cp:lem-split-strata} identifies the six reduced Artin terms.  The two
frames are exactly the two extreme classes in the relative cohomology
construction, as explained after Definition~\ref{cp:def-framed}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A three-weight category and its primitive kernel}

The proof does not assume the existence of a category of all mixed motives
over \(U\).  We use only the finite triangulated subcategory generated by
the objects which have just appeared.  Put
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_0=\langle\Q_U(0)\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_1=\langle\Q_U(1),\chi(1)\rangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_2=\langle\Q_U(2)\rangle.
 \label{cp:eq-pure-categories}
\end{equation}
The brackets mean finite direct sums and direct summands.  The middle
category is the rational Artin category generated by the degree-two cover.
If \(U_p\) is connected it is the subcategory generated by the trivial and
sign representations of its group of order two; if the cover splits, the
two displayed generators are isomorphic copies of the trivial object.
Either way it is semisimple (by Maschke's theorem in the connected case).

\begin{lemma}[Three-weight rigidity]
\label{cp:lem-three-weight-rigidity}
The triangulated subcategory of \(\DM(U)_\Q\) generated by the three
categories in \eqref{cp:eq-pure-categories} has a bounded heart
\(\mathcal M_U^{[0,2]}\), namely their extension closure.  The two motives
of Proposition~\ref{cp:prop-weight-calculation} belong to this heart.
For rational framed classes one has
\begin{equation}
 \ker\overline\Delta_{0,2}
 \;=\;
 \Ext^1_{\mathcal M_U^{[0,2]}}
       (\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2))
 \;=\;H^1(U,\Q(2))
 \;=\;0.
 \label{cp:eq-primitive-kernel-zero}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We first justify the heart.  The semisimple gluing criterion
\cite[Chapter~5, Section~1]{Goncharov1999} asks for negative morphisms
between the pure generators to vanish.  Equal-degree groups are direct
summands of \(H^m(W,\Q(0))\) for \(W=U\) or the finite \'{e}tale cover
\(U_p\), and vanish for \(m<0\).  Across one increasing Tate degree they
are direct summands of \(H^m(W,\Q(1))\), which vanish for \(m\leq0\)
because \(\Q(1)=\Gm[-1]\).  Morphisms in the decreasing direction have a
negative Tate twist and vanish by effectivity and cancellation: after
passing to a finite \'{e}tale cover to remove an Artin factor, they are
motivic cohomology groups of negative codimension, hence zero in the
higher-Chow description.  The only
two-step increasing group is
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\DM(U)_\Q}
   (\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2)[m])=H^m(U,\Q(2)).
\]

Let \(K=\Q(t_1,\ldots,t_6)\) be the function field of either rational
chart.  For \(m\leq1\), Garkusha's birational result and his
pure-transcendental invariance theorem give
\begin{equation}
 H^m(U,\Q(2))\cong H^m(K,\Q(2))
 \cong H^m(\Q,\Q(2)).
 \label{cp:eq-garkusha-reduction}
\end{equation}
Here we use Corollaries~3.10, 3.11, 3.13 and Theorem~3.14 of
\cite{Garkusha2023}.  The groups on the right vanish for \(m\leq0\) by
Beilinson--Soul\'{e} vanishing over \(\Q\).  This verifies the remaining
negative-Hom condition and constructs the finite heart.

For \(m=1\), motivic cohomology identifies the same group with the
weight-two part of algebraic \(K\)-theory:
\[
 H^1(\Q,\Q(2))
 =\gr_2^\gamma K_3(\Q)\otimes\Q.
\]
Borel's rank theorem says that \(K_3(\Q)\) has rational rank zero, so this
group vanishes.

We also need the strict filtration hypotheses, not merely the existence of
the heart.  Same-level extension groups are direct summands of
\(H^1(W,\Q(0))\), which is zero by the higher-Chow description.
For \(j>i\), both
\(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal P_j,\mathcal P_i)\) and
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathcal P_j,\mathcal P_i)\) have negative Tate
twist and vanish by the preceding effectivity argument.  The degree-zero
maps between distinct increasing levels were already shown to vanish.
Consequently extensions can be reordered uniquely by Tate degree; the
heart has the functorial strict filtration required in
Lemma~\ref{cp:lem-three-level-framed}.

That lemma identifies the primitive kernel with
\(\Ext^1_{\mathcal M_U^{[0,2]}}(\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2))\).
The heart-to-derived comparison in degree one identifies this with
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\DM(U)_\Q}
 \bigl(\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2)[1]\bigr)=H^1(U,\Q(2)),
\]
which the Garkusha--Borel calculation has just shown to be zero.  This
proves every equality in \eqref{cp:eq-primitive-kernel-zero}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Regge coproduct calculation}

For an oriented quadric simplex in projective three-space, Goncharov's
middle coproduct has one term for each of its six codimension-two faces.
Under the standard weight-one identification with multiplicative units, it
is
\begin{equation}
 \overline\Delta\,[Q,M]
   =\sum_e L_e\otimes A_e.
 \label{cp:eq-angle-length-coproduct}
\end{equation}
Here \(L_e\) and \(A_e\) are the oriented length and angle cross-ratios for
the same physical edge \(e\).  The order matters because we are working on
the projectively dual quadric \(Q_G\).  For complementary pairs
\(\{i,j,k,l\}=\{0,1,2,3\}\), restriction to the coordinate line
\(M_k\cap M_l\) has binary matrix
\(G_{\{i,j\},\{i,j\}}\) and gives the angle factor
\(A_{ij}\).  The quotient by that line has the complementary binary Schur
form and gives the length factor \(L_{kl}\).  Since the facets \(i,j\) meet
at the physical edge \(kl\), these are the two factors belonging to that
edge.  Goncharov's coproduct puts the quotient factor first, hence
\(L_{kl}\otimes A_{ij}\).
Indeed, if \(S\) denotes that Schur form on the quotient coordinates
\(k,l\), block inversion gives
\(S^{-1}=(G^{-1})_{\{k,l\},\{k,l\}}\).  The normalized dual vectors to
the facet normals are the vertices, so
\[
 \frac{(G^{-1})_{kl}}
      {\sqrt{(G^{-1})_{kk}(G^{-1})_{ll}}}=\cos\ell_{kl}.
\]
Inverting a symmetric binary matrix negates its normalized off-diagonal
entry.  Therefore the normalized off-diagonal entry of \(S\) is
\(-\cos\ell_{kl}\), exactly the same binary form that produces the length
cross-ratio below.
The signs are induced by the chosen ruling of \(Q\); there is no additional
alternating edge sign.  This is Goncharov's definition of the coproduct,
together with its Poincar\'{e}-dual description
\cite[the \(S_2(\C)\) cross-ratio example, the coproduct definition preceding
Theorem~4.5, and Proposition~4.9]{Goncharov1999}.

We use the ordered cross-ratio convention
\[
 r(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)
 =\frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}
        {(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}.
\]
Take affine slope on an oriented real two-plane.  The ordered isotropic
directions are then \(-\mathrm i,\mathrm i\), while two real rays meeting
at angle \(\phi\) may be written \(0,\tan\phi\).  A direct calculation gives
\[
 r(-\mathrm i,\mathrm i,0,\tan\phi)
   =\frac{1+\mathrm i\tan\phi}{1-\mathrm i\tan\phi}
   =e^{2\mathrm i\phi}.
\]
The homogeneous determinant formula for the cross-ratio gives the same
identity when one of the slopes is infinite.  Swapping the two isotropic
directions inverts the answer.

Consequently, if \(e\) is a physical edge, \(\vartheta_e\) its interior
dihedral angle, and \(\ell_e\) its length, then with the induced orderings,
\begin{equation}
 A_e=e^{2\mathrm i\vartheta_e},
 \qquad
 L_e=e^{2\mathrm i\ell_e}.
 \label{cp:eq-cross-ratio-phases}
\end{equation}
The two induced orientations are coupled: with the auxiliary induced
orientation held fixed, reversing the global ruling reverses exactly one of
them, so every tensor term changes sign, as does the oriented
\(S_4\)-generator.  In the proof we keep the ruling selected by \(w\) fixed
throughout.

Let \([\mathfrak m_G]\) and \([\mathfrak m_{G^R}]\) denote the two
\((0,2)\)-framed coefficient classes of the normalized relative motives.
Their \emph{universal dual Regge defect} is
\[
 \delta_U^\vee=[\mathfrak m_G]-[\mathfrak m_{G^R}]
 \in A\bigl(\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2)\bigr).
\]
The symbol \(\overline\Delta_{0,2}\) below is the reduced coproduct in this
relative coefficient coalgebra.  Its specialization is compared only
afterwards with the reduced coproduct in Goncharov's quadric-scissors
coalgebra.

\begin{lemma}[Regge invariance of the middle coproduct]
\label{cp:lem-coproduct-zero}
Let \(\delta_U^\vee\) be the difference of the two framed dual quadric
motives associated with \(G\) and \(G^R\).  Then
\[
 \overline\Delta_{0,2}(\delta_U^\vee)=0
\]
over \(U\), including the possibly nonsplit \(p\)-summand.
Moreover, for every complex point \(s:\Spec\C\to U\), the corresponding
dual quadric-scissors defect satisfies
\[
 \overline\Delta(\delta_s^\vee)=0
 \quad\text{in}\quad
 \bigl(S_2(\C)\otimes S_2(\C)\bigr)\otimes\Q.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The terms for the two fixed opposite edges agree individually, because
Regge fixes both their angle and length.  For the four moving terms use the
facet-pair order
\[
 (02,12,13,03)
\]
for the angle factors, and attach to them the complementary physical-edge
lengths in the order
\[
 (13,03,02,12).
\]
The second list is again a cyclic order around the two fixed opposite
edges.  Thus Theorem~\ref{cp:thm-regge-input} applies the same matrix \(R\)
to the two lists, while the entries with labels \(01\) and \(23\) are the
two fixed terms.  On a finite \'{e}tale cover choose half-phases
\[
 A_i=B_i^2,
 \qquad L_i=Y_i^2.
\]
Here the cover also includes the roots needed to recover lengths from the
angle Gram matrix.  To see explicitly that these roots are algebraic, let
\(C_{ij}=\operatorname{Cof}_{ij}(G)\).  If \(i,j\) are the endpoints of a
geometric edge, the dual-basis construction of the vertices gives, with a
sign fixed by the physical branch,
\[
 \cos\ell_{ij}
   =\frac{C_{ij}}{\sqrt{C_{ii}C_{jj}}}.
\]
Jacobi's complementary-minor identity says
\[
 C_{ii}C_{jj}-C_{ij}^2
 =\det(G)\det G_{\widehat{\{i,j\}},\widehat{\{i,j\}}},
\]
If \(\{k,l\}\) is the complementary pair, then
\[
 \det G_{\widehat{\{i,j\}},\widehat{\{i,j\}}}
 =1-g_{kl}^2
 =-\left(\frac{u_{kl}-u_{kl}^{-1}}2\right)^2,
\]
where \(g_{kl}=-(u_{kl}+u_{kl}^{-1})/2\).  Thus the square root of this
minor is already supplied by the known angle phase.  The identity therefore
expresses \(\sin\ell_{ij}\), hence
\(Y_{ij}=e^{\mathrm i\ell_{ij}}\), using \(w\), square roots of the
nonzero principal cofactors, and the phase belonging to the complementary
binary angle section.  All radicands are units on our chosen open, so
adjoining these finitely many roots gives a finite \'{e}tale cover.  If the
fixed cover \(U_p\to U\) is connected, include it as one more factor in
this fibre product.

Choose the connected component \(V\) of the resulting cover which contains the
physical choices \(B_i=e^{\mathrm i\theta_i}\) and
\(Y_i=e^{\mathrm i\ell_i}\).  The map \(V\to U\) is surjective: a connected
component of a finite \'{e}tale cover has open-and-closed image, and \(U\)
is connected.  When \(U_p\) is connected, the same argument shows that the
induced map \(V\to U_p\) is surjective.  There is a real open neighbourhood
\(\Omega\) of the given
tetrahedron on which both Gram matrices remain positive definite.  Its
phase image is a nonempty maximal totally real open subset of the
six-dimensional complex manifold \(U(\C)\): algebraically, the full phase
orientation cover maps finitely and dominantly to \(U\), forgetting only
the choice between \(p\) and \(p^{-1}\), and the six real angles are local
coordinates on the physical chamber.  A holomorphic function which
vanishes there is zero by the one-variable identity theorem applied
successively in local analytic coordinates; hence the image is Zariski
dense.  Choose the square-root branches analytically over a smaller simply
connected part of \(\Omega\).  Their lift
\(\widetilde\Omega\subset V(\C)\) is a nonempty maximal totally real open
set in the complex manifold \(V(\C)\).  A regular function on the integral
variety \(V\) which vanishes on \(\widetilde\Omega\) is holomorphic in local
coordinates and hence vanishes identically by the same successive identity
theorem.  Thus \(\widetilde\Omega\) is Zariski dense in the chosen
component \(V\).

Let \(R=\tfrac12J_4-I_4\) be the Regge matrix and put
\(H=2R=J_4-2I_4\).  The angle and length parts of the Regge theorem give,
first on the real spherical chamber,
\begin{equation}
 A_i^R=\prod_jB_j^{H_{ij}},
 \qquad
 L_i^R=\prod_jY_j^{H_{ij}}.
 \label{cp:eq-monomial-angle-length}
\end{equation}
Both sides are algebraic functions on \(V\).  Since \(\widetilde\Omega\) is Zariski
dense there, the identities hold algebraically over its function field.
We write a multiplicative group additively inside a tensor product; in
particular
\[
 [u^m]\otimes[v^n]=mn\,[u]\otimes[v].
\]
Since \(H^tH=4I_4\), this convention gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \sum_i L_i^R\otimes A_i^R
   &=\sum_{i,j,k}H_{ik}H_{ij}\,Y_k\otimes B_j\\
   &=4\sum_jY_j\otimes B_j
    =\sum_jL_j\otimes A_j.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Changing a chosen square root contributes only two-torsion, which
disappears over \(\Q\).

All functions in this tensor identity are regular units after the shrinking
made at the start of this section.  For an integral scheme \(V\), the map
\(\mathcal O(V)^*\hookrightarrow K(V)^*\) is injective, and remains so after
tensoring with \(\Q\); the same is true of its tensor square over \(\Q\).
Consequently the identity proved in the function field is already an
identity of the relative weight-one coefficient objects on the cover.

We finally descend the equality.  If \(V\to U\) is a finite \'{e}tale
cover with structure map \(f\), then on units
\[
 N_{V/U}\circ f^*=[\deg(V/U)].
\]
Thus \(f^*\) on rationalized units is injective, and its tensor square is
injective as well.  This descends the equality formed by the five split
channels (the four moving terms and the fixed \(q\)-term).  If \(U_p\) is
split, the \(p\)-channel is a sixth ordinary-unit channel and the same
argument with \(V\to U\) finishes the descent.  Suppose instead that
\(U_p\) is connected.  The surjective finite \'etale map \(V\to U_p\) has
an injective pullback on rationalized units by its norm, so the fixed
\(p\)-term first descends to \(U_p\).  For the deck involution \(\sigma\) of
\(\pi:U_p\to U\), put
\[
 e_-=\frac{1-\sigma}{2},\qquad
 \chi=e_-M_U(U_p).
\]
Using \(\pi^*\chi\cong\Q_{U_p}\), pullback on the two half-frame spaces is
\begin{align*}
 \pi^*:A_U(\Q(0),\chi(1))
     &\longrightarrow A_{U_p}(\Q(0),\Q(1)),\\
 \pi^*:A_U(\chi(1),\Q(2))
     &\longrightarrow A_{U_p}(\Q(1),\Q(2)).
\end{align*}
On either space, the normalized twisted trace---the \(\chi\)-isotypic
projection of transfer, divided by two---is a left inverse: trace after the
adjunction unit is multiplication by the degree, namely two, on the sign
summand.  The tensor product of these
two left inverses proves injectivity on the complete \(p\)-term.  That term
is identical for the two mates on \(U_p\), since Regge fixes both the
\(p\)-angle and its complementary length.  Its difference therefore
vanishes already over \(U\).  Equation
\eqref{cp:eq-angle-length-coproduct} now proves the relative motivic
assertion.

The calculation has taken place in the rationalized multiplicative unit
groups themselves, before applying any period or regulator.  Goncharov's
cross-ratio isomorphism identifies
\(S_2(\C)\otimes\Q\) with \(\C^*\otimes\Q\), and
\eqref{cp:eq-angle-length-coproduct} is also the coproduct formula in his
quadric-scissors Hopf algebra.  Pulling the same unit identity to any
complex point therefore proves
\(\overline\Delta(\delta_s^\vee)=0\) in the stated scissors target.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Vanishing of the universal framed defect]
\label{cp:thm-relative-defect-zero}
The universal framed coefficient class is zero:
\[
 \delta_U^\vee=0
 \quad\text{in}\quad
 A\bigl(\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2)\bigr)
\]
for the restricted three-weight heart \(\mathcal M_U^{[0,2]}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{cp:prop-weight-calculation} places the two framed motives,
and hence their difference, in the heart of
Lemma~\ref{cp:lem-three-weight-rigidity}.  Lemma
\ref{cp:lem-coproduct-zero} says that their difference is primitive.  The
primitive kernel is zero by \eqref{cp:eq-primitive-kernel-zero}.
\end{proof}
